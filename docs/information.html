KNOW WHAT TO DO

Protect What Matters.

Tap a topic to open the steps. This is the day-of list for when a fire threatens your neighborhood.

⌂ Inside Your Home Do these last, right before you walk out the door.
  1. Close every window and door. Leave them unlocked so firefighters can check inside.
  2. Take down curtains and shades. Move furniture to the middle of the room, away from windows and walls.
  3. Shut off the gas at the meter. Put out pilot lights too. Skip this if you are not sure how, and tell a firefighter when you see one.
  4. Turn off the air conditioner and furnace. Stop ceiling fans as well, so smoke is not pulled into the house.
  5. Leave the interior lights on. Your house stays visible to firefighters in heavy smoke.
♧ Outside Your Home Clear the yard and get the car pointed at the street.
  1. Bring in anything that burns. Patio furniture, cushions, doormats, toys, umbrellas, and firewood.
  2. Move the propane away from the house. Shut off the tank and move the BBQ and spare cylinders.
  3. Connect hoses and fill buckets. Do not leave sprinklers running. They use up the water pressure firefighters need.
  4. Turn on the exterior lights. So the house is visible in smoke or at night.
  5. Load the car and back it in. Kit inside, facing the street, windows up, keys with you.
  6. Leave a ladder out. At a corner of the house, for roof access.
  7. Close the attic and ground vents. Use pre-cut plywood or vent covers you already have.
♡ Animals & Pets Find them early. Loading animals takes longer than people expect.
  1. Keep pets close. Carriers, leashes, and food ready before the fire gets near.
  2. Move horses and livestock early. Before an evacuation order, with a trailer and a destination already lined up.
  3. Pack their supplies. Food, water, medications, and vaccination records, with your own kit.

Never leave an animal behind. If one has to be left behind, tell a firefighter or animal control where it is.

↗ Leaving the Area Go early, and take the route with the least smoke.
  1. Leave when evacuation is recommended. Do not wait for a mandatory order, and do not wait for someone to knock.
  2. Take a route away from the fire. Keep two ways out of the neighborhood, and pick the one with the least smoke.
  3. Dress for embers. Long sleeves and pants in cotton or wool, sturdy shoes, an N95 mask, goggles, and a hat.
  4. Drive with the windows up. Headlights on, vents closed, air on recirculate.
  5. If you are trapped, call 911. Say where you are, turn on every light, and shelter in a room away from exterior walls with the doors closed.
  6. Check on neighbors. Make sure they know what is happening and have a way out.

Once you are out, stay out. Do not go back for belongings until officials say it is safe.

Steps adapted from the City of San José Fire Department's Pre-Evacuation page.
